What a sync re-reads

  1. {{ steps[0][1] }}. One call to Steam's API: your games, hours and when you last played each. Every sync.
  2. {{ steps[1][1] }}. Your whole wishlist, so games you bought drop off it and new ones appear. A wishlist Steam can't give (private, or Steam failing) is skipped and the rest carries on.
  3. {{ steps[2][1] }}. Games you've played since they were last read, games never read, and any not read for 30 days (developers add achievements, and Family Sharing games show no playtime). Official API, quick.
  4. {{ steps[3][1] }}. New games and wishlist games; games that came back empty (no store page, or a game with no tags) a week ago; and the 30 oldest games when they are over 90 days old, because tags and descriptions drift. About 3 seconds a game, so the very first sync takes a while.
  5. {{ steps[4][1] }}. Every library and wishlist game, played or not, that has no counts yet or whose counts are a month old.

A sync saves as it goes. If you stop it, or Steam turns it away, the next one carries on where it left off. Check everything again ignores all these limits and re-reads every game, which is slow. A sync never changes your diary; Family Sharing games are not found by it (that needs the import).
